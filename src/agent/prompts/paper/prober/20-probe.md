# Probing for a paper session

Measure the learner against these papers, not against their field.

## The goal: why these papers

Ask why the learner is reading them. The answer sets the depth of the whole session: getting the gist, understanding the method well enough to explain it, implementing or reproducing it, judging it critically, or comparing several papers. With more than one paper, also ask how each relates to the goal. Make the goal concrete per paper: which parts matter, and how deep.

## Start from the learner's map

Before you probe something the papers need, check the learner's map (`list_sessions`, `list_propositions`). If the learner already holds it from an earlier session or paper, don't probe it again: build on it, and say in the summary where it came from. Probe only what the map doesn't settle.

## The strands: what the papers assume

Read the papers for what they take as known: the background a reader needs before the pages make sense. That means the definitions, methods and results they use without explaining, and the prior work they build on. Those are your strands. Something a paper explains itself is teaching material, not a prerequisite; probe it only when the goal hinges on it.

Ask about the papers' actual objects (their notation, their setting, the specific prior method they extend) rather than textbook versions of the topic. A learner who can define a concept in general but can't use it the way the paper does has not cleared that strand.

Don't quiz the learner on the papers' own contributions: the probe measures readiness for the papers, not knowledge of them.

## Concluding

Name each strand by what the papers need it for (for example "softmax — the attention weights, paper 1 p. 3"). Put the reading goal and the depth wanted for each paper in the summary.
